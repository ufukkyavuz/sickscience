# Reference analysis: INO "INO nasıl doğdu?" reel (instagram.com/reel/De91m9MRtVC)

The reel is 114 s long, 720×1280, with a licensed music bed. I sampled frames at 0, 4, 14, 24, 34, 46, 58, 70, 82, 94, 104 and 112 s and did not review the sound by ear.

## Mechanisms worth borrowing
1. **The ending comes first.** Frame 0 shows the full spread, and the film then builds it. The viewer sees the destination and watches how it gets there.
2. **One page that keeps growing.** Every beat adds to the same page and the camera pans down, so nothing is replaced. The page becomes the evidence.
3. **Numbers as the spine.** "1 Keşif", "8 yıl", "2 kat" and "15 yıl" are written big and highlighted in yellow. Everything else is small handwriting.
4. **Writing as storytelling.** Crossing out ("laboratuvar → insanlar", "makyaj mı? bakım mı?") delivers the turn of the story with no VO needed.
5. **Real photos as glued specimens.** Petri dishes and raw materials appear as round "FIG." photos stuck to the page.
6. **The end card has the same structure** as the cover, which gives the film a loop feel.

## Where it looks artificial (what "more realistic" means for us)
- The page is a flat vector white with no paper fibre, lighting, curl or shadow.
- The pen is a floating 3D render. No hand holds it, and its tip doesn't touch the line it draws.
- Lines appear by wipe. The ink has no pressure variation or bleed, and the line width is perfect.
- The photos are flat circular crops with a drawn outline, not physical prints.
- The camera moves are digital pans and zooms with no depth of field, and the light never changes.
- The yellow highlight is a perfect rectangle.

## Our "realistic" version (a direction for SickScience)
- **A real lab notebook on a white lab bench**, filmed or generated as photography: a grid lab notebook (the kind with "Project / Date / Witness" fields) lying on the bench, with a pen, a pipette, a petri dish and a little gel-serum nearby. Soft daylight, real depth of field, paper curl, page shadow, slight grain.
- **A real hand writes.** The pen tip touches the paper, the ink is fresh and slightly glossy, the pressure varies, and the handwriting is imperfect.
- **Highlights in product colours** with a real highlighter (violet, red, green, cobalt) that is slightly uneven. The numbers and the turns of the story are highlighted.
- **Real prints taped in.** Polaroids or prints of lab or plant petri dishes are held on with washi tape and labelled "FIG. 01…".
- **Crossing out is the turn of the story:** "scientist → marketer?" strikethrough, "lab → home".
- **Closing:** the page is finished, the camera lifts up, and the real four products are on the bench beside the notebook (photographed in the scene, not pasted).
- **Length:** INO runs 114 s. Ours is about 50 s organic plus a ≤20 s ad cut. The VO alternates between Merve and Polen.

## Production limits
- The reliable way to get a real hand writing on real paper is generated video. Veo/Kling redraw text badly, so the handwriting must not depend on the model. **Proposed method:** generate a realistic still of the notebook page (empty, Nano Banana Pro Unlimited), then write the handwriting on it with code as stroke-by-stroke animated ink with pressure, bleed and grain, and add a real hand-and-pen plate (one generated clip of a hand writing, with motion tracked to the stroke tip) or keep the hand out of frame with the pen's shadow only. The text stays exact and editable.
- These are things I inferred from silent frames. Music, SFX and VO timing I did not hear.
