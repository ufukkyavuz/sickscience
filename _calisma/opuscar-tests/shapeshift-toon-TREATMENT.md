# ShapeShift: "Hello, tech-neck" (Mid-century Cartoon, talking presenter)

## Brief
- **Duration and format:** about 19.5 s; 1080×1920 at 24 fps (Meta Reels and Stories). Feed and Square are crops of this master.
- **One sentence:** tech-neck from looking down is real, and ShapeShift is the routine for it, used upward, twice daily.
- **Voice:** Kokoro af_heart (US, warm). Every line was whisper-checked and passes.
- **Lines** (site wording only):
  1. "Looking down at your phone all day?"
  2. "Hello, tech-neck."
  3. "Meet ShapeShift, the V-Line Jaw Defining Serum."
  4. "Use it upward, twice daily."
  5. "92% saw a more sculpted jawline."
  6. "Chisel your jawline in 4 weeks."
- **Product:** the REAL ShapeShift photo (ERH_7470B, client PNG). It is the only photographic object in a drawn world, a mixed-media hero, held in her drawn glove hand at face depth. Scale: the bottle (12 cm) is about 2/3 of her hand length.
- **Style overrides for the brand:**
  - Founders Grotesk replaces the style's script and slab faces.
  - Palette: a lilac ground, a deeper lilac floor, a magenta accent on her top, ink near-black. The product keeps its own purple.

## Three structures
1. **Classroom rules card:** "3 rules for your neck", numbered medallions. Too slow for 19 s, and the "rules" would be invented advice.
2. **Do / don't split:** looking down versus chin up, in two colour blocks. Clear, but it has no product moment until the end.
3. **Problem → presenter fix (chosen):** a held presenter, head bowed to her phone. The phone is swapped for the product with one hand action on a beat. A call-out shows the upward stroke; a medallion stamps 92%; a proud chin-up; an iris-out on the bottle. It follows the medium's "limited animation points the eye" rule: the body stays held, the hand is the step.

## Logline and arc
A woman stuck looking down at her phone looks up, swaps the phone for ShapeShift, and finishes chin-up and proud.
- **Setup:** head bowed, neck creased.
- **Turn:** she looks up and says hello to the problem; the hand swap.
- **Ending:** chin up, product beside her jaw, iris-out onto the bottle (bookend: the phone glow was the first circle, the iris the last).

## Benchmark
- **Learn from 1950s classroom films:** direct address, one sentence per picture, limited animation, off-register colour.
- **Don't take:** any characters, lettering, the style demo's presenter or product, or music.

## Cue map
- **Tempo:** 120 bpm, swing feel (brushes, walking pizzicato bass, vibraphone chords, finger snaps). Bars are 2 s.
- **Sections:**
  - A (0–2.9): sparse vibraphone plus a clock tick.
  - Silence (2.6–2.9) before the look-up.
  - B (2.9–4.9): brushes come in.
  - C (4.9–8.8): full combo, with a vibraphone glissando on the reveal at 4.9.
  - D (8.9–11.1): half-time feel.
  - E (11.2–16.9): back to full; it moves up a step at 14.5 for the payoff.
  - End (16.9–19.5): one final vibraphone chord rings, and the iris "shhk" lands at 16.9.
- **Foley:**
  - phone screen tap at 0.6
  - neck creak/cartoon "crick" at 2.2
  - head-up swish at 2.9
  - phone pocket swish at 4.6
  - glass clink plus sparkle at 4.9
  - two soft "swoop" glides at 9.3 and 10.1 (upward strokes)
  - rubber stamp at 11.3 (medallion)
  - tag pop at 14.7
  - iris shhk at 16.9
  - paper slap for the end card at 17.3
- **Mix:** voice compressed on top; music ducked about 8 dB under the voice; foley unducked; −14 LUFS.
