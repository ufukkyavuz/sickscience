We're telling this story for the first time. SickScience began with two geneticists. Polen Koçak-Denizci and Merve Yıldırım-Canpolat. They met in a chemistry and biology class in 2009. Then came fourteen years of working together. Both earned biotechnology PhDs at Yeditepe. Polen was also a research scholar at Harvard–MIT Health Sciences and Technology. Merve focused on plant-derived exosomes in cancer research. They studied stem cell production, regenerative medicine, and exosome applications in cancer research. International patents. Twenty to thirty peer-reviewed papers. But there was a problem. Merve's mother had mesothelioma. No treatment was available. And their research was still preclinical. After her mother passed, they finished and published the research. Then they made a decision. Science couldn't stay in the lab. It needed to reach people's homes. At the center? Exosomes. Tiny carriers, about one-thousandth of a cell's size, delivering coded messages between cells. Think delivery drones. Their source? Plants. Pineapple, garlic, wheat, and radish. Thousands of plant messages. One hundred percent plant-based and vegan. Then came NX35. A biomimetic platform: exosome separation, room-temperature stabilization, and their plant library. No cold chain. Why beauty? Clinical-level science, in formulas people could use at home. Everyone in beauty says they're a scientist. Most are marketers. Then a beauty executive saw their data. 'That's sick!' Their answer? 'No, that's science.' And SickScience was born. ShapeShift for neck and jawline. NetWork for face. PowerCycle for hair. DropOff for body. Four products. Fourteen awards. Oprah, InStyle, and Esquire. Science, but never boring.
